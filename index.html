Slide 02 · Bottom Line

What leaders need to know

Strategic takeaway

Diabetes and periodontitis are not parallel comorbidities — they are mechanistically coupled. Treating one without the other leaves residual systemic risk and cost on the table.

2-way
Causal directionality

Hyperglycemia worsens periodontitis; periodontitis worsens glycemic control.

~0.43%
HbA1c drop after therapy

Mean effect from RCTs / meta-analyses after periodontal treatment.

Why it matters now

  • High co-prevalence in adult populations
  • Guideline-level consensus for co-management (EFP–WONCA)
  • Oral care is a low-cost adjunct to cardiometabolic risk control
  • Pathway biology suggests multi-disease prevention opportunities
  • Healthcare systems undervalue dental–medical integration

Diabetes → mouth

  • Higher risk, severity, and progression of periodontitis
  • Impaired neutrophils / macrophages; cytokine imbalance
  • Excess osteoclast activity; impaired bone repair
  • Xerostomia, caries risk, candidiasis, delayed healing

Mouth → metabolism

  • Chronic periodontal load raises systemic inflammation
  • Insulin resistance and glucose intolerance signals
  • Possible nitrate-reducing microbiota / NO pathway impairment
  • Potential amplification of microvascular complications
Evidence note: Longitudinal data show diabetes often precedes periodontitis; RCTs show treating periodontitis improves glycemic markers — supporting both epidemiologic and interventional linkage.
Slide 04 · Molecular Architecture

Core pathways connecting the two diseases

01
AGE–RAGE axis (KEGG hsa04933) AGEs from hyperglycemia engage AGER/RAGE → NADPH oxidase, PKC, MAPKs → NF-κB → IL-1, IL-6, TNF, IL-8, adhesion molecules. Positive feedback via RAGE upregulation.
02
Type 2 diabetes core (KEGG hsa04930) TNF and stress kinases (JNK/IKKβ/PKC) impair IRS-1 → insulin resistance. β-cell ROS and PDX-1 loss degrade insulin secretion.
03
Innate immunity (Reactome) RAGE signaling intersects TRAF6–NF-κB, TAK1–IKK, IL-1, and TLR/MyD88 cascades — the same modules activated by periodontal pathogens.
04
Bone remodeling RANK/RANKL/OPG imbalance + diabetes-impaired osteoblast / MSC function → net bone loss and poorer regenerative capacity.
05
Tissue-level effectors Gingival fibroblasts express RAGE; AGE exposure drives IL-6/IL-8. Vitamin D can attenuate this cytokine response in experimental models.
06
Open Targets signal (T2D) High-association genes include KCNJ11, ABCC8, GCK, PPARG, INSR, GLP1R, AKT2 — metabolic machinery that sits upstream of inflammatory vulnerability.
Slide 05 · Peer-Reviewed Evidence

What the literature establishes

Epidemiology

  • Uncontrolled T2D elevates periodontal risk and severity
  • T1D and gestational diabetes also implicated
  • Diabetes associated with tooth loss and oral complications
  • Independent association affirmed in EFP consensus

Mechanisms

  • Oral dysbiosis + barrier dysfunction
  • AGE–RAGE, oxidative stress, immune senescence
  • Altered adaptive/innate immune balance
  • Impaired bone regeneration pathways

Intervention

  • Periodontal therapy → ~0.43% HbA1c reduction
  • Potential healthcare cost savings in diabetic cohorts
  • Treatment effect heterogeneity remains a research priority
  • Co-management pathways recommended

Consensus posture

EFP–WONCA Europe (2023): periodontitis is independently associated with diabetes and other NCDs; dentists and family doctors should collaborate on early detection and lifestyle risk management.

Evidence limits

Causality from periodontitis to diabetogenesis is plausible but not fully settled. Complication amplification (e.g., renal) is stronger observationally than experimentally proven as independent causation.
